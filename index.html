Mount Rainier National Park. Visitation 2016–2025

Crowds Return to Mount Rainier: Ten Years of Visits and a Reservation Experiment

Mount Rainier National Park is busier than it has been in decades. Recreation visits grew from about 1.15 million in 2013 to more than 1.6 million in 2025, and 2023 was the park's busiest year since 1977. Long lines at entrances and full parking lots led the park to test timed-entry reservations at Paradise and Sunrise in 2024. In 2025, only Sunrise needed reservations while construction paused the Paradise system. This story asks: did reservations change how many people came? The numbers suggest they did not. Visits dipped 3.2% in 2024 and rose 0.9% in 2025. That matches the park's own goal, which was to spread visits out during the day, not to cut them.

D1 How This Story Was Made

Visit numbers come from the National Park Service Visitor Use Statistics (IRMA) Annual Park Recreation Visitation report for Mount Rainier, accessed November 1, 2026. These are recreation visits only. I calculated the percent changes. Timed-entry details come from the NPS and news sources listed below.

Reservations were not the only change during these years. Construction also affected the Paradise area in 2025, so this story does not claim that reservations caused any change in visits.

Data changes

I used only 2016–2025 from the full 1904–2025 report. Percent changes were calculated as (this year − last year) ÷ last year, rounded to one decimal place. In the bar chart, each bar's height is visits ÷ 6,000, rounded to a whole number.
